# Daggerheart Core Rules — GM Context Module

## System Philosophy

Daggerheart is fiction-first. Mechanics serve narrative. Only roll when fictional uncertainty exists and the outcome matters. Avoid rolls when success is obvious, failure has no consequence, or direct narrative resolution serves the scene better.

## Action Resolution: Duality Dice

Players roll 1d12 Hope + 1d12 Fear, add relevant trait/proficiency/domain bonuses, compare to GM-set Difficulty.

- **Hope die higher** → player gains 1 Hope. Result trends toward momentum, control, opportunity, cleaner success.
- **Fear die higher** → GM gains 1 Fear. Introduce complication, danger, pressure, or narrative cost even on success.
- **Matching dice** → critical success with especially strong outcome.

## The Six Stats

Agility, Strength, Finesse, Instinct, Presence, Knowledge. Each has a modifier used in relevant rolls.

## Hope

Players' resource. Gained when Hope die is higher. Spent to enhance rolls, activate class features, avoid consequences. Represents momentum, fortune, narrative agency.

## Fear

GM's escalation currency. Gained when Fear die is higher on player rolls, through certain adversary features, or through fiction granting narrative advantage to danger.

**Fear is not punishment — it is pacing and escalation.**

Spend Fear to:
- Activate adversaries outside ordinary flow
- Trigger adversary reactions
- Intensify attacks or worsen consequences
- Introduce reinforcements or reveal hidden danger
- Force difficult choices or alter the environment
- Interrupt player assumptions

Fear spending should create **visible fiction**, not invisible arithmetic. Players should feel what changed. Fear is strongest when used to alter scene direction rather than merely increase damage.

## Spotlight

No fixed initiative. Spotlight moves by fiction, urgency, consequence, and opportunity. After a player action resolves, spotlight may pass to another player, remain if fiction supports it, or shift to GM consequences. The GM uses Fear, adversary features, and fiction to justify spotlight changes — not rigid turn order.

## Range

Narrative, not grid-based. Categories: **melee/close**, **near**, **far**, **very far**. Position matters only when fiction meaningfully changes available actions, risks, or opportunities.

## GM Principles

- Present meaningful danger and uncertainty — do not oppose players
- Consequences move scenes forward; failure creates new fictional states, not dead ends
- Ask questions; let player answers establish details, relationships, memories
- Rulings over rules: prefer fast judgments that preserve tone, pacing, and fiction
- When rules are ambiguous, choose what best supports dramatic continuity
