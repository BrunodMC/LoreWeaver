# Daggerheart Core Rules — GM Context Module

Checked against the Daggerheart SRD 2.0 (2026-08-25). Page numbers refer to the SRD.

## System Philosophy

Daggerheart is fiction-first: use the fiction to drive mechanics, then connect the mechanics back to the fiction. Rulings over rules. Only call for a roll when the move is difficult or risky; if success would be trivial or failure boring, it just succeeds (p. 47).

## Action Rolls: Duality Dice (p. 47–49)

The player rolls two d12s, a Hope die and a Fear die, sums them, and adds modifiers:
- the relevant **trait** modifier
- any **Experiences** they spend Hope to use (+X each)
- an **advantage die** (+d6) or **disadvantage die** (−d6); never more than one, and they cancel one for one
- bonuses from features, cards or other effects

Proficiency is **not** added to action rolls; it sets how many damage dice a weapon rolls.

Compare the total to the Difficulty:

| Result | Outcome | Currency | Spotlight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success with Hope | "Yes, and…" | PC gains 1 Hope | Stays with the players |
| Success with Fear | "Yes, but…": success with a cost or complication | GM gains 1 Fear | GM takes it |
| Failure with Hope | "No, but…": fail with a minor consequence | PC gains 1 Hope | Swings to GM |
| Failure with Fear | "No, and…": fail with a major consequence | GM gains 1 Fear | Swings to GM |
| Critical Success (dice match) | Automatic success with a bonus | PC gains 1 Hope **and clears 1 Stress** | Counts as "with Hope" |

A critical success on an attack deals critical damage: roll normally, then add the maximum possible result of the damage dice.

**Reaction rolls** (resisting an attack or hazard) work like action rolls but generate no Hope or Fear and trigger no GM move. A critical reaction roll ignores the effect but grants no Hope and no Stress clear.

**Group action:** one PC leads; each helper makes a reaction roll; the leader gets +1 per helper success and −1 per helper failure.
**Tag Team (3 Hope, once per player per session):** two PCs roll, choose one result for both. With Hope: all involved gain a Hope. With Fear: the GM gains a Fear per PC involved.

## Traits (p. 4)

Six character traits: **Agility, Strength, Finesse, Instinct, Presence, Knowledge.** Starting modifiers are assigned from +2, +1, +1, +0, +0, −1.

## Hope (p. 49)

PCs start with 2 Hope, hold at most 6, and keep it between sessions. Spend Hope to:
- **Help an Ally:** roll an advantage die for their roll (only the highest advantage die counts)
- **Utilize an Experience:** add its modifier to a roll (1 Hope per Experience)
- **Initiate a Tag Team Roll:** 3 Hope
- **Activate a Hope Feature:** class Hope features cost 3 Hope

## Fear (p. 49, 87–88)

The GM starts a campaign with **1 Fear per PC**, holds at most 12, and keeps it between sessions. The GM gains Fear when a PC rolls with Fear, when the PCs take a rest, and when a feature says so.

Spend Fear to:
- interrupt the players, seize the spotlight and make a move
- make an additional GM move
- use an adversary's or environment's Fear Feature (paid on top of any Fear spent to seize the spotlight)
- add an adversary's Experience to a roll

Fear spent should create **visible fiction**, not invisible arithmetic: new adversaries, a powerful spell or transformation, an environment turning hostile. Don't hoard it.

Fear budget by scene:

| Scene | Examples | Fear |
|---|---|---|
| Incidental | Catching up, gathering info, resupplying, resting | 0–1 |
| Minor | Travel sequence, small skirmish foreshadowing trouble | 1–3 |
| Standard | Substantial battle, perilous travel, tense social scene | 2–4 |
| Major | Battle with a Solo or Leader, character-defining scene | 4–8 |
| Climactic | Showdown with the arc's villain, epic set piece | 6–12 |

## GM Moves (p. 86–87)

Make a GM move when a player rolls with Fear, fails a roll, does something with unavoidable consequences, gives you a golden opportunity, or looks to you for what happens next. Use soft moves on rolls with Hope and hard moves on rolls with Fear.

- **Critical Success:** let them describe it, then offer an extra opportunity or advantage.
- **Success with Hope:** let them describe it, show how the world reacts.
- **Success with Fear / Failure with Hope:** minor consequence: an adversary attacks, the PC marks a Stress, a new threat appears, or the stakes rise.
- **Failure with Fear:** major consequence: immediate danger, focus of several adversaries, separated from the party, or a lost opportunity.

Example moves: show how the world reacts; make an NPC act on their motive; dangle a PC's goals; signal an off-screen threat; reveal an unwelcome truth; force the group to split; make a move the characters don't see; show collateral damage; shift the environment; spotlight an adversary; capture something important; use a PC's backstory against them; take away an opportunity permanently.

The GM rolls a **d20** for adversary attacks: d20 + attack modifier against the PC's **Evasion**. A natural 20 succeeds automatically and adds the damage dice's maximum to the damage.

## Spotlight (p. 46–47)

No initiative and no fixed number of actions. After a move resolves, the spotlight goes to whoever a mechanic points at, whoever the fiction focuses on next, or someone who hasn't had it in a while. Optional: an action tracker of tokens per player (3 recommended).

## Damage, HP and Stress (p. 50)

Damage is compared to thresholds: below Major → mark 1 HP; Major or above → 2 HP; Severe or above → 3 HP; reduced to 0 → none. (Optional massive damage: twice Severe → 4 HP.) Damage types are physical (phy) and magic (mag); resistance halves, immunity ignores.

PCs have 6 Stress slots at creation. Marking the last Stress makes a character Vulnerable; if a character must mark Stress but can't, they mark 1 HP instead.

## Range (p. 51)

Ranges are narrative but named precisely:

| Range | Distance | Movement in danger |
|---|---|---|
| **Melee** | touching, a few feet | — |
| **Very Close** | 5–10 ft | can move into Melee as part of an action |
| **Close** | 10–30 ft | can move into Melee as part of an action |
| **Far** | 30–100 ft | Agility Roll to move safely into Melee |
| **Very Far** | 100–300 ft | Agility Roll to move safely into Melee |
| **Out of Range** | beyond Very Far | usually can't be targeted |

Group effects target everything within Very Close of one point, unless stated otherwise. Attacks through partial cover have disadvantage.

## GM Principles (p. 85)

- Begin and end with the fiction.
- Collaborate at all times, especially during conflict: antagonism belongs only in the fiction.
- Fill the world with life, wonder, and danger.
- Ask questions and incorporate the answers.
- Give every roll impact.
- Play to find out what happens.
- Hold on gently.

Key practices: keep the story moving after every roll; cut to the action; tell players what their characters would know; ground the world in motive (NPCs act from goals and desires); reframe rather than reject. Pitfalls: undermining the heroes, letting scenes drag, insisting on a single solution, overplanning, hoarding Fear.
