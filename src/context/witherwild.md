# The Witherwild — Campaign Frame Context Module

Condensed from the Witherwild campaign frame: SRD 2.0 (2026-08-25) p. 184–189, verified against the core rulebook (1st ed., 2025) p. 254–261. The two texts are identical apart from one extra session-zero question in the book (included below). Designed by Carlos Cisco, Rowan Hall & Spenser Starke. Complexity rating: 1 (light).

## How to use this module

1. **The world bible outranks this module.** This file is the published frame; the bible is this table's campaign. Where they disagree, follow the bible and don't "correct" it back toward the book.
2. **Party knowledge comes from the bible, not the book.** The frame presents much of its lore as player-facing. This table keeps some of it secret. Never put a fact in party-facing output unless the bible marks it known and not `||hidden||`.
3. **Frame material the bible doesn't mention yet** (named gods, the Fanewraith, Kreil Dirn, Alula…) is fair game for *suggestions*, labelled as frame material. It is never established campaign fact.
4. **Expect divergence.** Tables rename, soften, relocate and keep secrets. A campaign may run its own opening instead of the frame's inciting incident, change how certain the veil cure is, or keep the cause of the endless spring hidden from the party. Check the bible before leaning on any frame detail.

This file is generic on purpose. It lives in a public repository, so campaign-specific divergences and DM secrets belong only in the world bible.

## Premise

Haven invaded the wild land of Fanewick to farm a rare flower that cures a plague. To grow more, its Archmage forced the land into eternal spring. Unchecked growth became the **Witherwild**, a corruption that warps plants, beasts and people into hungry, overgrown monsters. The PCs are unlikely heroes from humble beginnings, caught between a dying empire and a wounded wilderness.

**Tone & feel:** adventurous, dynamic, epic, heroic, thrilling, uncanny, whimsical.
**Themes:** cultural clash, ends justify means, grief, people vs. nature, transformation and change, survival.
**Touchstones:** *Princess Mononoke*, *The Legend of Zelda*, *The Dark Crystal*, *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind*.

## Timeline

- **Centuries ago:** Haven's founders, wanting freedom from the Faint Divinities, killed **Shun'Aush the Granite Ophid**. The method is forgotten. They built their home behind his stone remains, which became the city walls, inscribed **"The Godless Gate"** (original meaning lost; old tomes hint at the deicide).
- **Generations of prosperity:** while the god's scales lay undisturbed.
- **The plague:** deeper tilling and mining raised the god's stone dust. **Two years ago** the **Serpent's Sickness** broke out; over half of Haven has died.
- **The invasion:** Archmage Phylax found that crimson lady's veil cures it. Haven invaded Fanewick, and under Phylax's guidance plucked the **Reaping Eye** from **Nikta**. Spring became endless.
- **The past year:** Haven drained the bogs into veil plantations. The endless spring curdled into the Witherwild.

## Lore

### Haven
Once the region's dominant power. It has centuries of safety behind serpent-bone walls, so culture, art and progress sit alongside grim, ordered life. It's now a city of statues: plague victims petrified mid-agony, a "cenotaph to a once-great power." Officials quarantine the early-stage sick, with little effect. Desperate citizens carry or eat any flower and snake sheddings as folk cures. Wealth is old and inherited (highborne). It has a large **wizarding school** that trains fighters and healers; the Reaping Eye sits in a **vault beneath its central tower**. The **Haven Army** is mostly warriors and School of War wizards, carrying grief for a dying homeland. Its ports trade with distant lands. Its spies infiltrate Fanewick communities to gather intelligence and shape opinion.

### Fanewick and the Wicklings
Wild, untamed, long avoided by outsiders. The woods are dark and twisting, and innocent-looking fields hide bogs that swallow armies, artillery and all. The **Wicklings** are hardy folk who survive by strict, unique customs (some shared, some secret) that grant safe passage through deadly terrain and divine territories. **Knowledge is currency:** an economy of gifting and trade where wisdom is collateral for a weapon or a warm meal, so the learned (loreborne) are the wealthiest. Some Wicklings now work Haven's flower fields in exchange for protection behind Haven's fires, which burn back the Witherwild. Rebel groups resist the occupation. Seaside communities are small and local.

### The Serpent's Sickness
1. Hacking cough of dust like powdered bone.
2. A scaled rash that cracks the skin in a snakeskin pattern.
3. Hardening: flesh, bone, soft tissue, then organs ossify.
4. Once in the organs "the serpent" moves so fast that victims **petrify where they stand.**

Anyone can carry it, Havenites especially. It stiffens movement and leaves limited time without treatment.

### Lady's veil
Small white flowers in sunny patches across Fanewick, long used in herbal teas. **One bloom in ten thousand is crimson**; the crimson ones have specific ceremonial uses. Crimson lady's veil is the only known treatment for the Sickness. Endless spring hasn't changed the ratio, so Haven farms the drained bogs at massive scale, and Fanewick reeks of a **sickly sweet smell**.

### Nikta, the Great Owl, Shepherd of the Seasons (female)
The most powerful and most widely acknowledged Faint Divinity. She kept Fanewick's cycle by turning her eyes on the land, first for ripening, then ruination. With only the **Sowing Eye** left, she forces everlasting spring. Some Wicklings believe the Withered are Nikta's wrath, sent to drive out Haven and reclaim her Eye.

### The Witherwild
Corruption empowered by the endless spring. Some say it began in the forest, others the bogs. Plants wake hungry, animals swell to immense size with exaggerated teeth, tusks and claws, and even prey animals become formidable. Trees warp and hunt; vines strangle. A **Withered person** becomes a nightmarish hybrid, body intertwined with plant or beast, their personality slowly replaced by the drive to consume until their personhood is finally lost. Nothing stops the growth for long. Fire holds it back locally.

### The weeks of day and night
The sun stays up for a full week, then sets for a week of night. Many merchants refuse to travel at night. Day and night have entirely different soundscapes and creatures. Many plants live a whole life cycle in one week:
- **Sun rose vines** climb thornily to the canopy over a day-week and wilt under the moon.
- **Night bloom** is a bioluminescent flower that smells of burnt sugar and decay and blossoms only at nightfall, lighting patches of forest.

### The gods of Fanewick (Faint Divinities)
Incarnate, local and present. They wander the land and live in homes and villages, and many communities and large families have their own small god or tutelary spirit. Worship is a negotiation: gods court mortals with small miracles, services, tricks and curses, and mortals rely on their blessings to travel and work safely. They're capricious, likely to lure travelers off paths to their ruin. Opinions on the Witherwild vary: some revel in it, many lesser gods fear the imbalance. There are hundreds, if not thousands. Named in the frame:

**Shun'Aush the Granite Ophid** (male) is dead, but his revenge is the Sickness.

## Frame cast and hooks (optional; not in play unless the bible says so)

- **Archmage Phylax** (gender unspecified): High Magus of Haven, discoverer of the veil cure, and architect of the Eye theft. Whether the party knows about the theft is up to the bible.
- **Kreil Dirn** (male): Haven's beleaguered, unpopular spymaster; roots out insurgents and watches Haven itself. Privately disagreed with the Eye theft and feels grimly vindicated. Mercurial, with possible hidden motives. Has a vast informant network.
- **The Fanewraith** (female): mysterious leader of a secretive, dangerous rebel group. Her plan is to find Nikta and **pluck out the Sowing Eye** to end the endless spring, with no thought for the long-term consequences.
- **Alula:** a treetop village; Kreil suspects it's the Fanewraith's base.
- **Frame inciting incident:** Kreil invites the party to Haven to stop the Fanewraith. Open questions: is his information right, what's his own agenda, and will the party bring her to justice, join her, or go for the Reaping Eye instead?

## Mechanic: corruption from the Witherwild

- Adversaries and environments can gain the extra type **Withered**. Describe how the Witherwild changed their look or behavior.
- When a PC takes **Severe damage** from a Withered source, the GM places a **Wither token** on that PC. The PC then rolls their **Fear Die**. If the result is **≤ their Wither tokens**, they gain a **scar**, clear all tokens, and describe a permanent Witherwild change.
- At **session end**, clear all Wither tokens; the GM **gains that much Fear**.
- A PC who **dies with Wither tokens** is permanently taken over by the Witherwild.
- Needs about 20 tokens (Fear tokens work).

## Origins and archetypes (for NPCs and PCs)

| Community | In the Witherwild |
|---|---|
| Loreborne | Fanewick's wealthiest: hunters, historians, artisans who trade knowledge |
| Highborne | Haven's inherited rich; insulated from the Sickness, never immune |
| Ridgeborne, Underborne, Wildborne | Raised in Fanewick's deadly wilds; secret customs for safe passage; some forced into Haven's flower fields |
| Orderborne | Current or former Haven Army: violence carried alongside grief |
| Slyborne | Fanewick rebels against the occupation; may doubt their leaders' methods |
| Seaborne | Small local Fanewick seaside groups vs. Haven's big trading ports |
| Wanderborne | Found in both lands; travel choked by occupation and Witherwild; their own stance on the invasion |

- **Clanks:** Haven builds them of iron and steel, Fanewick of wood and stone.
- **Fungril:** some in the forests have grown noticeably larger.
- **Drakona, fauns, firbolgs, infernis:** horns growing faster and longer.
- **Galapa, ribbets:** bog families displaced by the invasion.

Class notes:
- **Druids, rangers and sorcerers** are common in Fanewick: their tie to nature is strained by the Witherwild.
- **Warriors and wizards** dominate Haven, with School of War wizards in the army.
- **Vengeance guardians** are typical of Wicklings seeking revenge or expulsion.
- **Syndicate rogues** connect to Haven's spies.

## Generation guidance

The frame's principles, as rules for anything generated for this campaign:

- **Paint the world in contrast.** The nightmarish can charm and the beautiful can terrify. Withered growth is gorgeous and deadly. Haven is grim but also cultured. Gods are capricious; mortals are devoted. Lean on dualities: day/night weeks, two nations, growth/decay.
- **Show true danger.** The worst losses aren't wounds: homes, families, communities, beliefs. Threaten what PCs' backstories make them care about.
- **Offer alternatives to violence.** Compassion can free a corrupted beast from the Witherwild. What threatens both nations could unite them. Always leave a non-violent path open.
- **Multidimensional allies and adversaries.** Allies hide darkness; villains sometimes do right for wrong reasons. Not every Havenite is a villain; not every Wickling is virtuous. Give everyone a motive grounded in grief, survival or faith.
- **Make it personal; treat death with weight.** Everyone has lost someone to the Witherwild or the Sickness, and taking a life has consequences.
- **Texture to reach for:** the sickly-sweet smell of veil fields; statues in Haven's streets; Haven's fires burning back the green; tribute stacks and offerings; night-bloom light; animals too large; houses regrowing branches; bone-dust coughs; snake sheddings worn as charms.

### Texture seeds (frame session-zero questions)
Good prompts for inventing details, NPC backstories, or rumors:
- What dangerous animal comes out only during the week of night?
- What unique trait (look, smell, taste) does anything Witherwild-tainted have?
- What superstitions surround traversing Fanewick in the long nights?
- What beautiful thing has come out of the Witherwild, and whose view of the corruption did it change?
- *(Book only)* A Fanewick native's secret taste for a Haven commodity; a Havenite's craving for a Fanewick delicacy.
- Which gods side with Haven to preserve the endless spring, and which with Fanewick? How has the Witherwild changed a given god's temperament?
- Who have they lost to the Sickness? What rumor were they ordered to plant? What kindness did an enemy show them? What knowledge did they trade for something terrible?
